How we know a drawing is right, and good

Claude verifies its own work first: correctness is proven by the machine every time, and quality is measured and judged by independent AI reviewers. You only see drawings once they pass all of that, and then we iterate together.

Every drawing, every time

A drawing that fails layer 1 or 2 is never shown as finished.

1

The connections are exactly the netlist

A separate checker ignores how the drawing was made, rebuilds what connects to what from the drawn lines, dots, pins and symbols, and compares it with the circuit.

catches: missing wire, accidental short, dotless T, wire through a pin, wrong supply label, hidden connected pin
built94 independent test cases; every deleted wire, dot or pin and every relabelled port caught; Appendix A examples pass
2

Nothing overlaps, everything is on grid

No wire through a part, label or symbol; no two parts or labels touching; every pin and corner on the 0.1 inch grid.

catches: label printed over a wire, symbols colliding, off-grid geometry
builtparts, symbols, labels and pin names against each other and against wires; grid; sheet edges. Each check catches a deliberate break. Found two engine bugs on the first run
3

It follows drafting conventions

Rule checks for what engineers expect: ground points down, supply up, signal flows left to right, rail-to-ground parts vertical, decoupling capacitor next to its pin.

catches: upside-down ground, signal flowing backwards, a pull-up drawn sideways
builtnextsupply and ground direction, flow, vertical rails, pull-ups, series lines, decoupling: each break is a finding. Tested fixes come with the Phase 0b findings harness
4

It is easy to read

Measured, with limits: crossings, bends, wire length, parallel wires squeezed together, junction dots too close, labels far from their part, wasted space, page shape.

catches: the two dots one step apart in case 3, the tight parallel wires in case 5
builtchecked by you laterlimits flag exactly the weaknesses you saw in cases 3 and 5, and the margin bug in all three; provisional until calibrated on your ratings

Every release, on a whole test set

These prove the engine is reliable, not just one drawing.

5

Stress tests at scale

Thousands of circuits generated from real patterns (dividers, pull-ups, op-amp stages, transistor drivers, boards with sensors). The same circuit written in a different order must give the same drawing. A small edit must only move nearby parts. Windows and Linux must produce identical files.

catches: crashes, rare layouts that break, drawings that jump around after one edit
builtnext300 generated circuits per test run (1,000 checked once); reorder and respelling give identical drawings; Windows/Linux check in CI. Edit stability waits for incremental mode (Phase 0b)
6

A blind AI judge

Fresh Claude agents, who never saw the code, get shuffled images from our engine and from SKiDL, netlistsvg and schemdraw, a written rubric, and one question per pair: which would you publish?

catches: drawings that pass every rule and still look wrong
nextchecked by you laterkit being built by a second agent (rubric, blind packs, tally); several independent judges must agree; your ratings later confirm it matches your taste
7

Real agents doing real tasks

Claude Code and Codex get a plain request ("draw the 555 blinker"). Pass only if the final circuit equals the reference, every layer above passes, and it took 3 rounds or fewer.

catches: tools that confuse agents, agents changing the circuit to make a prettier picture

Order of work

Claude first, you once it is confident

1. Claude builds, then runs layers 1-6 on its own. Drawings that fail any rule or that the AI judges reject go back for fixing, without asking you.

2. Only when everything passes does Claude hand you the set, shuffled and blind, with the judges' verdicts hidden.

3. You rate them. Where you disagree with the judges, Claude tightens the rules and the rubric, so the next round needs you less.
